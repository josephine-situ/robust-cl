\begin{frame}{Formulation}
  \textbf{Nominal}:
  \[
    \min_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\; c^\top x \quad\text{s.t.}\quad \hat f(x;\theta^\ast)\le b .
  \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Noisy labels $\Rightarrow$ different $\theta^\ast(\delta)$ $\Rightarrow$ infeasible
      decisions.
    \item \textbf{Robust}: feasible against \emph{every} reachable model.
  \end{itemize}
  \[
    \min_{x\in\mathcal{X}} c^\top x
    \;\;\text{s.t.}\;\; \max_{\delta\in\mathcal{D}} f(x;\theta^\ast(\delta))\le b,
    \quad \theta^\ast(\delta)=\arg\min_{\theta\in\Theta_{\text{feas}}}\mathcal L(\theta;X,y+\delta)
  \]
\end{frame}

% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{The headline change: one $\mathcal{D}$, three methods}
  \small
  Previously each method carried its \emph{own} notion of uncertainty --- CP a
  localized bootstrap, the wrapper a plain bootstrap, Robust Reg.\ a box$\cap$L1
  set. A difference between methods was then partly a difference in what each was
  handed.
  \medskip

  \textbf{Now all three face the same set}, and differ only in what they \emph{do}
  with it:
  \[
    \mathcal D_c = \{\delta: |\delta_i|\le\varepsilon_c,\ \lVert\delta\rVert_1 \le
      \texttt{budget\_frac}\cdot n\,\varepsilon_c\},
    \qquad \varepsilon_c = \varepsilon_0\cdot\mathrm{sd}(y_c)
  \]
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lll@{}}
    \toprule
    Method & What it does with $\mathcal D$ & Dial \\
    \midrule
    Robust Reg. & robustifies the \emph{fit}       & $\texttt{label\_eps}$ \\
    Wrapper     & chance-constrains a sample of it & $\alpha$ \\
    CP (ours)   & separates it \emph{lazily}       & $\tau$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \medskip
  \footnotesize
  $\texttt{budget\_frac}=0.5$ and $\varepsilon_0$ are shared constants, not knobs.
  $\varepsilon_0$ is deliberately \textbf{not} pinned to Robust Reg.'s calibrated
  $\varepsilon^\ast$: that would make $\mathcal D$'s definition depend on one
  method's tuning and force stage-1 CV to run sequentially.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Why $\mathrm{sd}(y)$, and not a residual-based radius}
  \small
  $\delta$ is added to labels \textbf{before} training and the model is retrained.
  It is not a post-hoc perturbation of predictions --- so the radius belongs in
  \emph{label space}.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\mathrm{sd}(y)$ is model-free, computable before any fit, and identical
      for all three methods.
    \item An out-of-fold \emph{residual} radius would make $\mathcal D$ depend on
      model class and hyperparameters --- circular, since Robust Reg.'s own model
      would define the set it is robust to, and $\mathcal D$ would silently move
      whenever model selection was re-run.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \textbf{Consequence, stated plainly.} Gastric toxicity labels are percentile
  ranks, so $\mathrm{sd}(y)$ is $0.2882$--$0.2887$ across all five --- measured, not
  assumed --- against $2.617$ for OS in months. That is a \emph{feature}:
  $\varepsilon_c=\varepsilon_0\,\mathrm{sd}(y_c)$ is then the same \emph{rank}
  shift in every outcome, which is what makes a shared coherent draw a coherent
  statement. OS, in raw months, gets its own scale.
  \medskip

  \textbf{And the residual estimator buys nothing here.} Measured out-of-fold on
  the five toxicities: $\mathrm{sd}$ $0.249$--$0.293$, $q_{0.9}$ $0.398$--$0.441$
  --- just as flat across outcomes as $\mathrm{sd}(y)$ is. It would add model
  dependence for no discrimination. Retained as a one-flag ablation; both logged.
  \medskip

  \textbf{No coverage claim} is made anywhere. This is a calibrated scale, not a
  conformal guarantee; the deliverable is \emph{comparability}.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{Draws are vertices of $\mathcal D$, not interior points}
  \small
  A scenario is a draw $\delta_b$: $\pm\varepsilon_c$ on $m=\texttt{budget\_frac}\cdot n$
  randomly chosen rows, $0$ elsewhere.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Robust Reg.'s adversary attains its inner max at exactly such a vertex.
    \item Sampling the \emph{interior} (uniform draw, then L1 projection) spreads
      mass thinly over all $n$ rows and would hand CP and the wrapper a
      systematically \textbf{weaker} adversary than Robust Reg.\ at the same
      $\mathcal D$ --- confounding the very comparison the shared set enables.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \textbf{Coherence} --- one flag, all three methods:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Coherent}: one standardized direction $u$ shared across outcomes,
      scaled per outcome as $\delta^c=\varepsilon_c u$. Inside every outcome's own
      $\mathcal D_c$, all six models moving together.
    \item \textbf{Incoherent}: an independent $u_c$ per outcome.
    \item Vacuous on synthetic (one outcome) --- verified: identical draws,
      identical $x^\ast$ for every method.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{The defect this fixes: CP was not converging}
  \small
  \texttt{results/gastric/cp\_trace.csv}, worst violation by iteration:
  \[
    0.0128 \to 0.0070 \to \cdots \to 0.0064_{(15)} \to 0.0233_{(17)} \to 0.0091
  \]
  It never converged --- it exhausted \texttt{max\_iterations} every time.
  \medskip

  \textbf{Cause.} Every iteration drew a \emph{fresh} scenario sample, while $d_0$
  was frozen from iteration 0's sample. The stopping rule compared iteration-$k$'s
  sample against iteration-0's sample $\times\ \tau$ --- \textbf{different samples},
  with sampling noise the same order as the signal.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item At $\tau=0.1$ the tolerance $0.00128$ was never reached.
    \item So $\tau^\ast=0.1$ and \emph{every} $\tau\lesssim0.5$ produced an
      identical 20-iteration run. The knob was inert.
    \item The earlier switch from absolute \texttt{dist\_tol} to relative $\tau$
      did not cure this: the problem was \emph{noise}, not units.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \textbf{Fix.} Draw the sample \textbf{once}. With $\delta$ fixed,
  $\theta(\delta_b)$ no longer depends on $x^\ast$; the models are trained once and
  every measurement refers to one fixed population.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{What the fixed bank does and does not buy}
  \small
  \textbf{Does} --- measured on synthetic:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{$\tau$ does something.} $\tau=0.8$ stops at 3 iterations
      (obj $-1.2767$); $\tau\le0.4$ runs 4 ($-1.2426$). CP \emph{converges} rather
      than exhausting its iteration budget.
    \item $d_0$ and every later measurement refer to the same population, so the
      comparison is meaningful.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \textbf{Does not} --- a claim we made and then had to withdraw:
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The violation trace is \textbf{still not monotone}:
      $0.132 \to 0.144 \to 0.073 \to -0.007$.
    \item Why: violation is measured at the \emph{current} $x^\ast$, which moves
      each iteration. A cut guarantees only that \emph{its} scenario is satisfied
      at the new $x^\ast$ --- not that the max over the rest fell.
    \item Cutting-plane theory gives monotone \emph{bounds}, not monotone
      separation values --- and eviction/pruning deliberately break even that.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{$d_0$ is a high quantile, not the max}
  \small
  \texttt{tolerance} $=\tau\cdot d_0$, with $d_0 = q_{0.9}$ of the iteration-0
  scenario distances.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item The \textbf{max} over $B$ draws grows with $B$ by construction --- so a
      max-based $d_0$ would stop $\tau$ transferring across bank sizes.
    \item And CP and the wrapper run at \emph{different} $B$ on purpose (next
      slide), so that transfer is not optional.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}rcc@{}}
    \toprule
    $B$ & $d_0 = q_{0.9}$ & $\max$ \\
    \midrule
     20 & 0.097 & 0.132 \\
     50 & 0.070 & 0.132 \\
    200 & 0.090 & 0.191 \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \medskip
  \footnotesize
  The quantile does not grow with $B$; the max climbs 45\%. Not perfectly flat
  ($B=20$ is 20 points), but the right estimator.
\end{frame}

% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{Why CP scales where the wrapper cannot}
  \small
  \textbf{The wrapper embeds all $P$ models} in the MIP, so $P$ is capped by MIP
  size. \textbf{CP embeds one extra scenario per iteration} and evicts/prunes
  cuts, so its master stays small at \emph{any} $B$. The bank is a separation
  \emph{pool}, not an embedded ensemble --- only the argmax becomes a cut.
  \medskip
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lrrr@{}}
    \toprule
    & adversaries & vars & constrs \\
    \midrule
    Wrapper & $P=20$  & 31{,}917 & 101{,}172 \\
    CP      & $B=200$ & \textbf{6{,}380} & \textbf{20{,}236} \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \medskip
  \textbf{Ten times the adversaries, in a fifth of the MIP.} CP's final master
  held 4 active cuts.
  \medskip

  This is the structural argument for lazy separation over chance-constraining,
  and it is now \emph{measured} --- both solvers log their size at termination.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{CP $\equiv$ a lazy wrapper at $\alpha=0$}
  \small
  Because draw $b$ is a pure function of $(\texttt{seed},b)$, the wrapper's $P$
  models are a genuine \textbf{prefix} of CP's bank --- nested, not merely
  identically distributed. So the two can be compared exactly.
  \medskip

  \textbf{Test.} $B=P=12$ on the identical prefix, objective un-robustified on
  both, CP at $\tau\to0$ vs.\ the wrapper at $\alpha=0$:
  \begin{center}\small
  \begin{tabular}{@{}lcc@{}}
    \toprule
    & $x^\ast$ & objective \\
    \midrule
    Wrapper ($\alpha=0$) & $(0.54468,\,0.70555)$ & $-1.25023$ \\
    CP ($\tau\to0$)      & $(0.54468,\,0.70555)$ & $-1.25023$ \\
    \bottomrule
  \end{tabular}
  \end{center}
  \[
    \max|\Delta x| = 0.0
  \]
  \medskip
  \textbf{Exact agreement.} CP is the wrapper's chance constraint at $\alpha=0$,
  separated lazily. $\tau$ is the difference --- and the reason CP can afford a
  bank the wrapper cannot embed.
\end{frame}

% ===========================================================================
\begin{frame}{Coherence is now a crossed factor}
  \small
  Previously coherence was an inconsistent per-method default: the wrapper was
  coherent, Robust Reg.\ was not, CP was coherent on gastric only. Now it is
  \textbf{one flag} crossed with all three methods.
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Wrapper}: coherent $\Rightarrow$ one shared $z_p$; incoherent
      $\Rightarrow$ per-constraint $z_{c,p}$.
    \item \textbf{Robust Reg.}: coherent picks \emph{one shared row set} $S$ per
      iteration --- ranked by mean \emph{normalized} $|r_i|$, so OS in months does
      not outvote percentile toxicities --- then shifts each outcome on $S$ in its
      own worst direction.
    \item \textbf{CP}: the bank's draws.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \textbf{Trade-off, not hidden}: with $\delta$ chosen jointly, Robust Reg.'s exact
  QP counterpart no longer applies (its inner max assumes the adversary optimizes
  one outcome alone), so the coherent arm retrains on shifted labels. Since
  coherence is crossed, the exact linear result stays available from the
  incoherent arm.
\end{frame}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{frame}{$\theta^\ast$ is calibrated \emph{per} coherence cell}
  \small
  A coherent draw moves every outcome together, so at a fixed radius it is the
  \textbf{stronger} adversary: the objective budget binds sooner, and
  \[
    \theta^\ast_{\text{coherent}} \;\le\; \theta^\ast_{\text{incoherent}}
    \quad\text{in general.}
  \]
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Reusing one $\theta^\ast$ across both would confound \emph{coherence}
      with \emph{more conservatism} --- exactly the confound the shared
      $\mathcal D$ exists to remove.
    \item Knobs are keyed \texttt{method@coherent} / \texttt{method@incoherent}.
    \item Costs $2\times$ stage 1; the scores checkpoint is keyed by the same
      string and resumable, so no cell is ever recomputed.
    \item Pareto frontier stays \textbf{coherent-only} --- crossing coherence with
      5 knob points $\times$ 30 draws risks the 12h wall for a second-order effect.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

    \item \textbf{No circularity}: the evaluator is independent of $\mathcal D$.

      win by construction.
    \item \textbf{Fixed}: the synthetic runner never passed a seed, so every

  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \footnotesize
  \textbf{Samestore cohort} (gastric): every reported number is a mean over test
  arms \emph{every} method could prescribe for, recomputed per draw. Feasibility
  is conditional on solvability, reported separately.
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Housekeeping}
  \small
  \begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{DLT-only dropped.} Per-mode samestore means the
      \texttt{all\_constraints} numbers never depended on whether
      \texttt{dlt\_only} also ran --- so this halves runtime and changes nothing.
      It also retires a real bug: \texttt{\_feas} selected the
      \texttt{all\_constraints} \emph{outcome} row regardless of mode, so the
      DLT-only panel never showed DLT-only joint feasibility.
    \item \textbf{Wrapper restored} to the gastric run, and its OS robustification
      turned \textbf{off} by default --- it always robustified the objective while
      CP did not, carrying conservatism CP never paid for (and $P$ extra
      embeddings).
    \item \textbf{Synthetic violation plot} now shows the violation itself, not the
      feasibility indicator: the indicator collapsed every infeasible method onto a
      flat 0 line (at $\sigma=0.5$: nominal $0.54$, RR $0.83$, wrapper $0.23$ all
      read as ``0.0'') and drew no-solution rows identically to
      solved-but-infeasible ones.
    \item \textbf{SLURM}: synthetic now runs \emph{first} and gates gastric
      (\texttt{afterok}) --- minutes of correctness checks before a 12h slot.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Summary}
  \small
  \textbf{What changed}
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{One shared uncertainty set.} $\mathrm{sd}(y)$-scaled
      box$\cap$L1, vertex draws, one coherence flag --- so a difference between
      methods is a difference in \emph{method}.
    \item \textbf{CP's scenario bank is fixed.} The oscillating trace was a
      fresh-sample-vs-frozen-$d_0$ mismatch; $\tau$ was inert below $0.5$ and now
      is not.
    \item \textbf{$d_0$ is a quantile}, so $\tau$ transfers across bank sizes.
    \item \textbf{Coherence is crossed}, with $\theta^\ast$ calibrated per cell.
  \end{enumerate}
  \medskip
  \textbf{Two results that stand on their own}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item CP at $\tau\to0$ $\equiv$ the wrapper at $\alpha=0$, \emph{exactly}
      ($\max|\Delta x| = 0$) --- CP is lazy separation of the same chance
      constraint.
    \item CP searches $10\times$ the adversaries in $1/5$ the MIP. That is the
      argument for separating rather than embedding.
  \end{itemize}
\end{frame}

\begin{frame}{Open questions}
  \small
  \textbf{1. Is $\varepsilon_0$ well-chosen?} \emph{The live question.}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item $\varepsilon_0=0.1$, taken from the label radius Robust Reg.\ has always
      used (\texttt{label\_eps}) and the weak end of its CV grid --- i.e.\ from the
      codebase's own precedent, not from either problem's DGP. Setting it from
      synthetic's known $\sigma$ is off the table (circular).
    \item \textbf{$\varepsilon_0=1$ was tried first and is not viable}: a
      29-percentile-rank shift on half the gastric arms, and $6\times$ the true
      noise on synthetic. CP could not converge against it in 20 iterations.
    \item Note $\mathrm{sd}(y)$ tracks the \emph{signal} spread on synthetic, not
      the noise --- so the same $\varepsilon_0$ is a relatively wider set there
      than on gastric. Sensitivity ablation over
      $\varepsilon_0\in\{0.05,0.1,0.2\}$; the claim to establish is that
      $\tau^\ast$, not $\varepsilon_0$, drives CP.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \textbf{2. Cutting planes per test point, or once over all anchors?}
  \begin{itemize}
    \item Per test point ($n=96$): tailored, but many different LPs.
    \item Over train anchors (now): offline, but $x^\ast_1..x^\ast_{10}$ may not
      represent test points.
  \end{itemize}
  \medskip
  \textbf{3. Add the WFP problem setting} --- the in-LP-context (``coupled'')
  formulation is described but has no data loader.
\end{frame}
